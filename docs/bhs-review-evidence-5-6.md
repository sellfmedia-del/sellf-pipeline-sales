## 6. Distinguish facts, declarations, hypotheses and client questions

The same analyst records its semantic scope and selected decision-changing claim checks, linked to actual draft paths/passages. A source fact needs adjudicated read-body provenance; a customer declaration needs a real brief passage; a hypothesis/client question needs a verification step. For an assessed highlight statement, its stored observation/brief/inference kind must agree with the assessment after the targeted correction. The analyst checks certainty consistently across the summary, narrative/SWOT, highlights and actions. This is no keyword/heading classifier and no fixed per-section claim quota.

Specific counterexamples are explicit: an unmentioned after-sales process does not prove a company weakness; a professional-sales restriction does not itself prove price power, channel control, margins or regulatory compliance. A quote appearing on a page does not independently substantiate every commercial inference. The analyst must condition an inference and state how to verify it, or identify the relevant actual supporting fact. Existing useful strategic hypotheses and sourced negative observations remain usable.

These checks bind the review to the retained evidence and expose its reasoning. They do **not** prove that an actual model will always judge commercial meaning correctly. Real business-quality, original Tartı repair and billed-cost acceptance remain user-run; no paid model was invoked by development checks.
