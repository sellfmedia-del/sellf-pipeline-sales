## Product understanding before comparison

Fresh conversation policies opt into `product-buyer-competition-v1`. The existing `begin_report` tool then accepts a versioned handoff containing the analyst's chosen scope and product/service groups. Each group carries the buyer, need, market, sales channel, commercial role and representative product/service analysis. Actual chosen public questions and read, adjudicated body sources bind the representative pages. Menu labels, an unreviewed downloaded page, an unread deep URL or a search snippet cannot stand in for that work.

The analyst chooses the relevant groups and narrow questions from the brief. There is no mandatory product count, B2B/B2C split, industry checklist, pricing field, competitor quota or automatic crawl. A one-service business may use its explanatory home page. Private revenue/margins remain client questions; a public price need not exist. Existing Tavily, free saved-source views and bounded specialist readers remain the available execution paths. No new provider or model, general scrape, extra budget or direct customer-data change is added.

## Match actual alternatives to the selected group

Each named competitor outcome is linked to one chosen group and an actual read alternative product/service page, with the existing comparison and commercial implication. The selected competition question and proof must support that actual page. A rival for another group cannot close the missing group; a sector name or search snippet cannot prove an alternative. One rival brand may be compared through different products where relevant. The final named comparison must retain an actual alternative-page source rather than cite only the audited brand's own product. A genuinely attempted unavailable product/alternative remains an explicit quality limitation but does not keep the conversation waiting for another peer; unread or fabricated assertions are never relaxed.

The same Claude conversation is instructed to assess product/buyer/market relevance, local availability, price position, software/service scope and the commercial decision only where relevant. These judgments are semantic responsibilities, not heading/keyword scores. Neither a structured answer nor an offline scripted test proves that a real model will always make the right commercial judgment.
